# Venue policy and submission profiles

Use this reference before selecting a template, setting a page budget, drafting with AI, or preparing a rebuttal. It is a dated evidence snapshot and a procedure for making a current profile, not a permanent rulebook. Research quality cannot guarantee acceptance.

## Resolve the exact target

Record `venue`, `edition`, `track`, `paper_type`, `stage`, and `submission_cycle`. A main research paper, short paper, benchmark track, position paper, workshop paper, and camera-ready paper may have different rules within one conference. An 11-page working draft is useful only as an internal scaffold when the actual submission limit is lower. Never label it submission-ready merely because its references start after page 11.

Read, in order: the specific call; author instructions; official current template; track supplements; submission form; official amendments. Check reviewer instructions to understand the criteria. Record each policy's effective date and source. If two official sources conflict, preserve both statements, identify the narrower or newer applicable policy, and mark any unresolved conflict. Do not silently choose the more permissive reading.

For a real submission, verify the profile again at outline lock, submission freeze, and camera-ready preparation. Missing rules remain `unverified`, not `false`, `unlimited`, or inherited from last year. Continue work that is independent of the missing rule; do not certify the package until material uncertainties are resolved.

If the author has not chosen a target, work on a clearly labeled internal draft and keep the venue profile unknown. Do not claim submission compliance. If an intended target is known to restrict generated prose, that restriction still applies; changing the label to “internal draft” does not make prohibited assistance compliant.

**Recheck on 2026-09-24** (official pages, retrieved for the companion `a-star-paper-review` skill):

- ICLR 2027: the main text of at most 9 pages is "strictly enforced. Papers with main text beyond the page limit will be desk-rejected"; the limit is 10 for the rebuttal revision and camera-ready. The **AI-use statement is a required section** and does not count toward the limit. Required disclosures include proposing hypotheses, designing experiments, implementing methods and interpreting results. Drafting and editing are recommended disclosures. Ethics (at most one page) and reproducibility statements are recommended. Hallucinated references led to desk rejection at ICLR 2026.
- ICML 2026: the main body is 8 pages and submissions over it are "automatically rejected". The impact statement is required; prompt injection means desk rejection.
- NeurIPS 2026: 9 content pages including figures and tables. A missing checklist means desk rejection.
- CVPR 2026: 8 pages excluding references. Hidden text or instructions and citations of non-existent material lead to rejection.

The ICLR 2027 [policy announcement](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/09/02/submission-policies-for-iclr-2027/) confirms that the next edition already has new submission policies. Never equate the calendar year of access with the conference edition. It also motivates evaluating correctness and significance separately; no formatting recipe resolves those judgments.

Use the executable JSON profile described in [tool-contracts.md](tool-contracts.md) for audit inputs. ```

Keep `ai_drafting_mode` separate from disclosure. Disclosure does not make a prohibited use permissible. For this optional planning record, select `drafting_allowed`, `editing_author_text_only`, `prohibited`, or `unverified` after checking the target's actual rule. These are summaries, not substitutes for task-specific conditions: permission for copyediting does not authorize substantive rewriting, and conditional drafting permission does not authorize every use. In editing-only mode, apply only the verified level of editing to author-supplied prose; separate research/outline critique and factual checks from manuscript text. Reproducibility prose is still manuscript prose when placed in the paper. Keep an activity log so disclosure describes what actually happened. Do not invent author review, verification, or approval.

Resolve the policy needed for the requested operation, without making unrelated unknown fields a global work stoppage. For example, an unknown appendix size limit does not block otherwise permitted abstract drafting. If the relevant AI permission remains unverified, state that uncertainty without treating it as a venue prohibition, and continue evidence organization, source verification, critique, and other work independent of that permission. Recheck unresolved requirements before claiming submission readiness. The no-target internal-draft provision above remains available; it does not override a known restriction for an intended target.

## Adapt the user's preferred structure

Honor the preferred abstract and introduction story, 3–4 meaningful contributions, a coherent related-work narrative, a lean section-aligned appendix (about 8 to 10 pages; see [appendix-and-prompts.md](appendix-and-prompts.md)), a compact discussion and conclusion, and only the prompts the claims need. No FAQ section unless the author asks for one (2026-09-21). These are author preferences, not venue rules.

Keep discussion and conclusion to one paragraph each by default, or one combined paragraph if requested and scientifically adequate. Expand or separate a required Limitations, Ethics, Impact, or AI statement as the venue demands. A background section may move to the appendix only if the main method remains understandable. Essential comparison with related work cannot disappear into an appendix. Use `Method` or `Methods` according to the actual content; grammatical number is not a review criterion.

A FAQ, when the author explicitly requests one, is optional reading and should be the last substantive appendix section. A required checklist may still follow if the venue mandates that ordering. Answer with evidence and pointers; do not use the FAQ to smuggle in indispensable assumptions, new claims, or absent experiments. A result unfavorable to the proposal remains reportable; persuasive writing explains the supported contribution and its boundary.

## Formatting, anonymity, and checklist preflight

1. Compile using the exact official style for the chosen stage. Check content-page boundaries in the rendered PDF, rather than counting source lines. Inspect both main paper and appendix at normal reading size.
2. Check paper size, embedded fonts, text extraction, links, bibliography, visible line numbers where required, cross-references, and figure legibility. Do not reduce margins, font sizes, line spacing, or hide content to fit a limit.
3. In review mode, inspect title page, acknowledgments, funding text, PDF metadata, file names, code comments, notebook outputs, demonstrations, URL destinations, image labels, and supplement archives for identity leaks. Preserve appropriate self-citations in neutral third person. Do not delete legally required third-party attribution from redistributed assets; resolve any conflict through permitted packaging.
4. Keep internal/private repositories distinct from the anonymous public artifact actually referenced by a submission. A private link reviewers cannot access does not provide reproducibility. Preserve an immutable release or submission snapshot if the venue requires one.
5. Answer each checklist item from actual evidence, with the requested section pointer and a candid qualification when needed. `No`, `not applicable`, `not measured`, and `not available` have different meanings. Never select every positive answer to create an appearance of completeness.
6. Prepare a release manifest with main PDF, supplement, source, bibliography, figures, data/code version pointers, and required forms. Freeze checksums of submitted artifacts. Recheck the venue's required order and names before marking the package ready.
